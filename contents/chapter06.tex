\chapter{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{chap:conclusion}

This chapter closes the thesis. Section~\ref{sec:conclusions} summarizes the proposed pipeline and the principal findings drawn from the five experiments, highlighting which feature configurations proved most robust across cohorts. Section~\ref{sec:futurework} then discusses the limitations of the present study and outlines directions for future work.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

This thesis investigated the binary classification of Parkinson's Disease from short hand-rotation videos recorded with everyday smartphones or tablets. A four-stage pipeline was constructed, consisting of MediaPipe-based 3D hand keypoint extraction, hand-crafted kinematic features (frequency, clarity, and four complementary amplitude representations), three feature-selection strategies (ANOVA F-value, L1-regularized Logistic Regression, and XGBoost gain-based importance), and a comparison of downstream classifiers including Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, and the feature-based AGCN-style model. Five experiments were carried out on two NTUH clinical cohorts (the 2020 and 2025 cohorts), with both LOOCV within-cohort validation and 2020-to-2025 cross-cohort transfer evaluation.

The principal findings are summarized as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Left-hand features are the strongest single-hand baseline.} The strongest single-hand baseline was left-hand XGB Importance + XGBoost, and the top-10 selected features aggregated across the three feature-selection methods were dominated by the left hand (25:5 and 19:11 in favour of the left hand). When AUROC, accuracy, and F1-score are averaged across 2020 LOOCV, 2025 LOOCV, and 2020-to-2025 cross-cohort transfer, this configuration also gives the highest overall hand-crafted baseline average ($0.69$), with mean AUROC, accuracy, and F1-score of $0.72$, $0.76$, and $0.58$, respectively. This asymmetry is consistent with the motor-reserve hypothesis, but because subject-level handedness was not recorded, alternative explanations---including population-level asymmetry in PD progression, recording-related confounds, or the particular features favoured by XGBoost importance---cannot be ruled out. The result is reported as an empirical left-versus-right asymmetry in feature discriminability rather than as confirmatory evidence for any specific mechanistic account.

    \item \textbf{Adding graph-aggregated joint-correlation features improves performance only marginally.} Augmenting the bilateral feature set with the 203-dimensional graph descriptor (Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage correlation, GCN-style aggregation, bilateral asymmetry, and block statistics) did not consistently outperform the strongest single-side baseline. Within cohort, the augmented configurations were on par with or slightly worse than the left-hand XGB Importance baseline; in cross-cohort transfer, AUROC improved only marginally (from $0.65$--$0.66$ to $0.67$--$0.68$) and without a stable improvement in accuracy or F1-score.

    \item \textbf{AGCN markedly improves within-cohort performance but does not transfer.} The AGCN reached AUROCs of approximately $0.98$ within each cohort under LOOCV. In cross-cohort transfer, however, AUROC dropped to $0.59$, below every hand-crafted configuration.

    \item \textbf{Adaptive graph topologies may be sensitive to the training distribution.} Because the AGCN learns its joint-wise adjacency from the training cohort, the discovered topology may not generalise across cohorts: the joint relationships that are informative for the 2020 protocol and population may not be equally informative for the 2025 protocol and population.

    \item \textbf{Small clinical datasets likely exacerbate the cross-cohort performance gap.} Under LOOCV, every fold trains on a near-identical distribution, so even high-capacity models such as AGCN can fit the training cohort well. Under cross-cohort transfer, the limited sample size may provide insufficient diversity to learn cohort-invariant patterns, and the resulting overfitting may amplify the distribution shift between the 2020 and 2025 cohorts.
\end{enumerate}

Taken together, these findings suggest that cross-cohort transfer performance---rather than within-cohort LOOCV---is the more informative benchmark for evaluating the clinical utility of a PD screening system, because it more closely mirrors the deployment scenario in which a model trained on one clinic's recordings must generalise to a different population or protocol. The graph-correlation-augmented configurations achieve the highest single cross-cohort AUROC among the hand-crafted methods ($0.68$), slightly above left-hand XGB Importance ($0.66$). However, when robustness is defined as balanced average performance across 2020 LOOCV, 2025 LOOCV, and cross-cohort transfer, the left-hand XGB Importance + XGBoost configuration has the highest overall mean ($0.69$) and therefore represents the most robust hand-crafted baseline identified in this thesis, while the AGCN offers higher within-cohort discrimination but fails to retain that advantage in the cross-cohort setting.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:futurework}

Based on the limitations and findings above, three directions are identified for future investigation:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Continue data collection to evaluate cross-year stability.} Larger, longitudinally collected cohorts~\cite{marek2011ppmi} would enable a more reliable estimate of cross-year generalization and would create headroom for higher-capacity models such as AGCN to overcome small-sample overfitting.

    \item \textbf{Lightweight the model and deploy it at the edge.} Because MediaPipe is designed for mobile hardware, the entire feature-extraction and classification pipeline can in principle be lightweighted (e.g., via quantization or distillation~\cite{jacob2018quantization}) and deployed on the user's device. This would enable real-time, at-home PD monitoring without the need for a server round trip.

    \item \textbf{Explore multi-modal large models combining hand gesture, gait, and voice.} PD affects multiple motor and non-motor modalities, and prior work has shown that smartphone-derived voice, finger-tapping, and gait features carry complementary diagnostic information for early PD identification~\cite{lim2025smartphone}. Extending this thesis's hand-rotation pipeline with gait and voice recordings within a unified multi-modal large model is therefore a promising direction: the complementarity of these modalities, together with the benefits of foundation-model pretraining, could mitigate the small-cohort overfitting that limited the AGCN in this study.
\end{enumerate}
