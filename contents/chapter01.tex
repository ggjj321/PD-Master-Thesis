\chapter{Introduction}
\label{chap:intro}

\section{Task}

This thesis investigates the binary classification of Parkinson's Disease (PD) risk from hand-rotation videos recorded with everyday smartphones or tablets. The clinical task we focus on is the pronation-supination test, in which an elderly subject repeatedly rotates a hand between palm-up and palm-down orientations in front of the front-facing camera. Given such a short video clip per hand, the system is required to output a single binary label: PD or healthy.

\section{Method Overview}

The proposed analysis pipeline consists of four stages. First, the MediaPipe Hands framework extracts a sequence of 21 hand keypoints from each video frame, producing a 3D skeletal trajectory for each hand. Second, three families of kinematic descriptors are computed from these trajectories: movement frequency, rhythmic clarity (regularity), and amplitude. Third, multiple feature-selection strategies are applied to reduce the resulting high-dimensional feature vector. Finally, multiple machine-learning models are compared as the final binary classifier, including Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, and an Adaptive Graph Convolutional Network (AGCN).

\section{Contribution}

The principal contribution of this thesis is to demonstrate that a binary PD-versus-healthy classifier built on hand-rotation videos captured by ordinary mobile devices can achieve up to roughly 80\% accuracy under Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV) on a clinical cohort.

\section{Clinical Background of Parkinson's Disease}

Parkinson's Disease is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder of the brain whose clinical manifestation is dominated by motor symptoms, including bradykinesia, resting tremor, rigidity, and postural instability. In clinical practice, neurologists rate symptom severity on a five-level scale derived from the Movement Disorder Society--Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (MDS-UPDRS), where stage~0 corresponds to a healthy subject and stages~1 through~4 correspond to increasing severity of PD. In line with the convention adopted throughout this thesis, stage~0 subjects are treated as the \emph{Healthy} class and stages~1--4 are merged into the \emph{PD} class. Among the many MDS-UPDRS motor items, this work focuses exclusively on the hand pronation-supination task, and casts the analysis as a binary discrimination between stage~0 and stages~1--4.
